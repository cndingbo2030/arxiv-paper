\section{A verifiable drift parameterization}
\label{sec:appendix-architectures}

The Cameron--Martin range condition is strong in infinite dimensions.
It can nevertheless be imposed directly, without making claims about
a particular neural-network family.

\begin{proposition}[Covariance-factorized drift]
\label{prop:factorized-drift}
Let
$\widetilde b_\theta:[0,T]\times\HH\to\HH$ be jointly measurable and
suppose that, uniformly in $t$,
\begin{align}
  \norm{\widetilde b_\theta(t,u)
        -\widetilde b_\theta(t,v)}_{\HH}
    &\leq L\norm{u-v}_{\HH},\\
  \norm{\widetilde b_\theta(t,u)}_{\HH}
    &\leq M(1+\norm u_{\HH}).
\end{align}
Define
\begin{equation}
  \drift(t,u)=\mathcal C^{1/2}\widetilde b_\theta(t,u).
  \label{eq:factorized-drift}
\end{equation}
Then $\drift$ satisfies \Cref{ass:drift} with the same constants.
Moreover, the projected drift \eqref{eq:projected-drift} satisfies
\begin{equation}
  a_N(t,u)
  =P_N\widetilde b_\theta(t,P_Nu),
  \label{eq:factorized-projected-drift}
\end{equation}
and the approximation quantity in \eqref{eq:drift-energy} is
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_N
  =
  \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q^\theta}
  \int_0^T
  \norm{
    \widetilde b_\theta(t,X_t)
    -P_N\widetilde b_\theta(t,P_NX_t)
  }_{\HH}^2\,\mathrm dt .
  \label{eq:factorized-energy}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal C$ is injective,
\[
  \norm{\mathcal C^{1/2}h}_{H_{\mathcal C}}
  =\norm h_{\HH}.
\]
Applying this identity to \eqref{eq:factorized-drift} proves the
Cameron--Martin Lipschitz and growth bounds.  Because $P_N$ is a
spectral projection of $\mathcal C$, it commutes with
$\mathcal C^{1/2}$.  Therefore
\[
  \driftN(t,u)
  =\mathcal C^{1/2}P_N
     \widetilde b_\theta(t,P_Nu),
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:factorized-projected-drift}.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:drift-energy} gives \eqref{eq:factorized-energy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Equation \eqref{eq:factorized-drift} is an architectural interface,
not a claim that an arbitrary pretrained score network satisfies
\Cref{ass:drift}.  A finite-dimensional network can be placed before
the fixed smoothing map $\mathcal C^{1/2}$, but a discretization study
must then verify uniform Lipschitz and growth bounds as $N$ changes.
Spectral normalization is one possible way to control finite-layer
operator norms \citep{miyato2018spectral}; it does not by itself prove
the Girsanov martingale condition or the local density bound in
\Cref{cor:local-mass-transfer}.
\end{remark}
